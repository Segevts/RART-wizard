What they said

These notes came with Test 2. They are not a score, and they are not Test 1.

Alby

Easier with generated polygons. You do not draw a polygon on each tile. You assign a value to each road section. No workflow break across the two generated-polygon days.

Overpasses: the generated polygon is one continuous shape, so the upper piece has to be deleted before the road underneath can be worked. A split from one RART to another, or within the same RART, creates a large polygon that has to be deleted and tagged again.

Ann

Easiest is the generated polygon. Most of the work is tagging: read the road, assign the RART, fix overlaps. No other problem on tiles that already had polygons, except multi-level overlaps.

On the new tool, a polygon can snap to another and merge into one shape that is hard to separate. She wants overlap handling improved on multi-level roads, and the straight split kept, because that is the one they use most.

Both methods can fuse polygons into one big shape. On the generated path the trigger is a split or an overpass. On the new tool the trigger is a snap-merge. Neither comment says the new tool is unfit.
